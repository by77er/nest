# Client-owned MCP credentials (2026-11-27)

[MCP credentials](../../docs/control-plane/api/clients/README.md#mcp-credentials): `{secret}` and `{authorize}` auth in a client's capabilities, and the client's `…/oauth` routes.

- **Not a one-way deploy, and no ordering constraint.** No new Durable Object class, table or column. BrokerDO's existing `oauth_grants` and `oauth_flows` rows hold the new records, under refs a previous release never writes (`client-oauth:<cli>:<entry>:<incarnation>`). Agent specs keep the existing `McpGrant` shape.
- **In the gap after the Worker deploys,** before every Durable Object runs this release (the usual minutes):
  - The new routes call new BrokerDO methods; on a BrokerDO still on the previous release they answer 503 `internal`, retryable (`newMethod`).
  - Every write and delete of a client's capability (any client's, the projects app's included) also revokes through BrokerDO. Until BrokerDO runs this release, each does its catalog part and then answers 503 `internal`, retryable; the same request again finishes. An app that writes its catalog at deploy (the projects app's sync) may see that for the gap's minutes.
  - The new public routes (`/v1/oauth/callback/<cli>`, `/v1/oauth/clients/<cli>.json`) answer as soon as the Worker runs this release. A BrokerDO of the previous release ignores the callback's owner, so the check that a state comes back to its own client's callback isn't in force until BrokerDO runs this release. That is harmless: that BrokerDO holds no client's flows, and an operator's state brought there completes as it would at the operator's callback.
  - Writing a capability with either new form to a CatalogDO still on the previous release is refused (400), but only by accident: its `checkCapability` refuses an `auth` object whose `client` equals its `oauth`, and both are undefined in `{secret}` and `{authorize}`. Its message ("the client secret and the token record must be different names") is misleading. It succeeds once CatalogDO runs this release. Its `putCapability` doesn't parse `auth` against its schema; its `load()` does, and skips such rows.
  - An agent whose BrokerDO is still on the previous release fails these calls with `forbidden` (`not a capability credential`), never sending a wrong bearer. An AgentDO of the previous release with a BrokerDO of this one works: the spec's grant shape is unchanged.
- **A `wrangler rollback`:**
  - CatalogDO logs and skips each capability holding `{secret}` or `{authorize}` (it can't parse them), keeping the rows. Templates listing one don't expand, so their spawns fail until the roll-forward, which reads the rows again.
  - BrokerDO refuses the derived refs as bearers, so these agents' calls fail closed. The stored OAuth records stay and serve again after the roll-forward.
  - A client capability deleted, or written with another URL or form, under the rolled-back Worker isn't revoked. Its records stay and still serve the agents spawned with them (they hold the old record and URL), but no new spawn gets them: a capability made again under the entry gets a new incarnation. After the roll-forward, `DELETE …/oauth`, or the next write or delete of the entry, revokes them. The same holds for a delete whose revocation answered 503 and was never sent again.
  - The previous release's client delete doesn't revoke a client's OAuth records. Nothing reaches them: their client id is gone, and a new client gets a new id. They stay in BrokerDO, and their tokens stay valid at their servers until those expire them; a refresh token may never expire (unverified per server). The person who authorized can revoke the app's access at the service.
- **No shim to drop.**
